# Recruiter outreach playbook

How the outreach is written, sequenced and checked. No outreach method guarantees a result. This one is built to raise reply rates and to stop avoidable losses.

## 1. How executive recruiters work
- Retained recruiters work for the client company, not the candidate. They respond when a candidate fits a search they have now or expect soon. So relevance and timing beat volume.
- They file good people for later. A clear, specific first message gets stored and found again. A vague one is forgotten.
- A referral from someone they know is read first. Always look for a warm route before sending cold.
- Most senior roles are never advertised. The recruiter relationship is the route in.

## 2. Positioning (do this before any outreach)
One sentence that a recruiter can repeat to a client:
`<Function> leader who <result with number> at <type and size of company>, now looking for <level and role> in <industry and region>.`
Back it with three proof points from the resume: scope (budget, P&L, headcount), a result with a number, and a hard situation handled (turnaround, scale-up, integration). No number in the resume means an `[ADD number]` marker, never a guess.

## 3. Message rules
**First email**, under 130 words:
1. Subject: `<Function> leader, <industry>, <region>: <one result>`. No "seeking opportunities".
2. Line one: why this recruiter, using one true and specific fact (their practice, a named placement, or the signal).
3. The positioning sentence.
4. Two proof points with numbers.
5. What they want: level, role, industry, region, work mode, timing.
6. One ask: a 15 to 20 minute introduction call, with two time windows.
7. Bio attached or linked. Nothing else.
Banned: flattery, life story, "I am passionate", "open to anything", apology, desperation, any mention of a withdrawn offer, more than one ask.

**Follow-up 1** (5 working days later), under 60 words: reply on the same thread, add one new fact (a result, a relevant market observation), repeat the ask.

**Follow-up 2** (10 working days after follow-up 1), under 60 words: offer something useful, such as a view on the market or a referral of another strong candidate for searches that do not fit. Recruiters remember sources.

**Breakup note** (15 working days after follow-up 2), under 40 words: "I will not chase further. My bio is on file if a fitting search comes up." Then stop. Re-contact only on a new signal or after 90 days with real news.

**LinkedIn note**, under 300 characters: who, one result, why them, no ask beyond connecting.

**Warm-intro ask**, under 80 words, to the mutual contact: who the recruiter is, why the fit, a two-line forwardable blurb, and an easy way to say no.

## 4. Call prep: standard recruiter questions
Prepare short, consistent answers for:
- Why are you looking now?
- What happened with your last role or last process? If asked about a withdrawn offer: answer in one calm sentence, no blame, then move to what they want next. Example shape: "We reached a verbal offer, the company then changed direction on the role. I am now focused on <target>."
- Target role, level, industry, company stage.
- Compensation: current and expected range, and what is flexible.
- Location, relocation, travel, notice period.
- Three referees who will speak for them, and whether they have been told.
- What would make them say no.
Close every call with: "Which of your current or upcoming searches could fit?" and "Who else should I be speaking to?"

## 5. Quality gate (every draft must pass)
- Names a true, specific fact about this recruiter. Would not make sense if sent to someone else.
- Every number and claim traces to the resume.
- Within the word limit. One ask only.
- No banned phrases. No mention of a withdrawn offer. Confidentiality note respected.
- Recruiter's level and practice match the target. Contact route is one the recruiter published.

## 6. Offer protection
A verbal offer is not an offer. Until a signed contract exists:
- Ask for the written offer within 24 to 48 hours, and ask who else must approve it (board, HR, finance, parent company).
- Ask whether anything is still open: budget sign-off, references, background checks, other finalists.
- Send a same-day thank-you email that restates the agreed title, pay, start date and next step. This creates a written record.
- Do not resign, announce, or stop other processes. Keep every other conversation moving until signature.
- Brief referees before they are called.
- If it goes quiet for two working days, call the recruiter or hiring manager, not just email.
- If an offer is withdrawn, ask politely for the reason and whether the door is open for other roles. Then log it and move on.

## 7. Weekly rhythm
- Day 1: review the report, send due follow-ups, check warm routes for new drafts.
- Day 2 to 3: send new outreach, a few a day, each personalised. Update tracker statuses.
- Day 4: calls and call prep.
- Day 5: one visible act on LinkedIn (a short post or comment on the candidate's field) so recruiters who look find a current, credible profile.
Review every two weeks: which tier and which message produced replies. Do more of that, change what gets none.
